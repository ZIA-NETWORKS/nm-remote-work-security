# NM SecureWork

**Practical remote and hybrid work security for New Mexico small businesses.**

Remote and hybrid work gives small teams flexibility, but it also spreads
company data across home networks, personal devices, and cloud accounts.
This repo collects the most common security mistakes New Mexico small
businesses make, along with simple, affordable fixes.

## Who This Is For

- Small business owners and office managers
- Solo consultants and freelancers
- Teams without a dedicated IT or security staff

## Common Mistakes at a Glance

| # | Mistake | Quick Fix |
|---|---------|-----------|
| 1 | "We're too small to be a target" | Treat security as a basic cost of business |
| 2 | Passwords only | Enable MFA and use a password manager |
| 3 | Unmanaged personal devices and home networks | Set a device policy and home Wi-Fi checklist |
| 4 | No VPN or secure access | Require a business VPN, avoid public Wi-Fi |
| 5 | No lost/stolen device plan | Enable disk encryption and remote wipe |
| 6 | Untrained staff and phishing | Short regular training, verify payment requests by phone |
| 7 | Messy cloud sharing | Least-privilege access, quarterly reviews |
| 8 | Untested backups | 3-2-1 backups, test restores twice a year |
| 9 | Ignoring legal obligations | Learn which rules apply, write an incident plan |
| 10 | One-time security setup | Quarterly security review |

## Quick-Start Checklist

- [ ] Turn on MFA for email and financial accounts
- [ ] Roll out a business password manager
- [ ] Enable automatic updates and disk encryption on all devices
- [ ] Confirm a backup exists and restore a test file
- [ ] Write a one-page incident response plan with phone numbers

## New Mexico Legal Notes

New Mexico's Data Breach Notification Act requires businesses that hold
residents' personal information to use reasonable security measures and to
notify affected people after a breach. Depending on your industry, HIPAA,
PCI DSS, or federal contractor requirements (NIST SP 800-171, CMMC) may also
apply.

> This repo is for general education and is not legal advice. Confirm
> current requirements with an attorney.

## Where to Get Help

- New Mexico Small Business Development Centers (SBDC)
- CISA (Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency)
- Local managed IT providers
- Your cyber insurance carrier
